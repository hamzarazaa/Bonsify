## Why merchants look for a Shopney alternative

Shopney's starting price of $99 a month makes it one of the most accessible ways to get an app live, and for merchants testing whether an app matters at all, that low bar is genuinely useful. Merchants tend to look for something else once push notification limits, template constraints, or the ongoing work of running the dashboard start outweighing the initial savings.

## What switching to Bonsify looks like

Bonsify runs the process in four phases: a free audit, the build, launch, and an optional support retainer. The audit is free and tells a merchant in days whether a native app is the right move, before any spend happens.

Apps are native, not templated, so they convert about 130 percent higher than mobile web (Venn Apps, 2025), with users spending 6.4 times longer per session inside them (BuildFire). Push, worth 20 to 26 percent open rates against 15 to 20 percent for email, is built into the launch phase with full segmentation, not left as a limited builder setting.

Switching from Shopney is a standard migration through Bonsify's app rescue service, which carries the store's existing setup into the new build rather than starting from scratch.

Pricing is a fixed quote after the audit, with no success fees or GMV percentage. It costs more than Shopney's entry tier, and it reflects a full build and team behind it.

Bonsify's scope also covers what comes before or instead of an app: store design, optimization, and platform migrations, under the same team.
