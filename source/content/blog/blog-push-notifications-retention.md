Push notifications are one of the highest-performing retention channels available to a Shopify store, and most stores aren't using them because they haven't built a native app or PWA that supports them. Push open rates run 20 to 26 percent, against 15 to 20 percent for email (industry benchmarks), a gap that compounds across every send when email is the only retention channel a store has.

## Why the gap exists

Email retention has been the default for so long that a lot of stores never seriously evaluate the alternative. But email competes for space in a crowded inbox, gets filtered by spam algorithms, and depends on a customer actually opening their email app. Push notifications land directly on a locked screen, no inbox competition, no spam filter, and a much shorter path between the notification and the customer noticing it.

That directness is what drives the higher open rate, and it matters more the more time-sensitive the message is. A restock alert, a price drop, an abandoned checkout reminder, all lose value the longer they sit unread, which is exactly the kind of message email is worst at delivering quickly.

## What push notifications are actually good for

Not every message belongs in a push notification. The channel works best for behavior-triggered messages tied to something specific: an abandoned checkout, an item back in stock, an order status update, a subscription about to renew. These are messages a customer is likely to want immediately, which is why they perform well.

Generic promotional blasts sent to every customer at once tend to underperform the 20 to 26 percent range and, worse, drive customers to disable notifications entirely. The channel's value depends on restraint as much as on the technology itself.

## Building a real push strategy, not just push access

Most app builders technically support push notifications, but access to the feature isn't the same as a strategy behind it. A real push strategy defines which events trigger a notification, how customers get segmented so the right people receive the right message, and a send cadence that avoids fatigue. That's setup work, not a toggle in a dashboard.

Push notifications work best as part of a broader system: install-to-purchase attribution to measure what's actually working, subscription and reorder programs for categories with repeat-purchase behavior, and a native app or PWA fast enough that customers don't abandon the session the notification brought them into. ## FAQ

**Why do push notifications outperform email?**
They land directly on a locked screen without competing for inbox space or facing spam filtering, which shortens the path between a message being sent and a customer seeing it.

**What kinds of messages work best as push notifications?**
Behavior-triggered messages tied to specific events, abandoned checkout, back-in-stock, order status, tend to outperform generic promotional blasts.

**Can too many push notifications hurt a store?**
Yes. Over-sending or sending generic content risks customers disabling notifications entirely, which is why cadence and targeting matter as much as access to the channel.

**Do PWAs support push notifications the same way native apps do?**
PWAs support push with more platform limitations than native apps, which is one factor in choosing between the two formats.

**How is push notification performance measured?**
Through install-to-purchase attribution, tying notification sends to actual purchase behavior rather than open rate alone.
