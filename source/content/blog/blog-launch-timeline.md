The timeline to launch a Shopify mobile app depends heavily on which model a store chooses, and the range is wide: a self-serve template builder can go live in days, while a fully custom native build typically takes weeks to a few months, with a done-for-you build usually landing somewhere in between depending on scope.

## Self-serve builder timelines

Template builders are built for speed. Choosing a template, connecting the store, and configuring basic screens can happen in a matter of days, sometimes less, since the app is assembled from pre-built components rather than developed from scratch. That speed is the main tradeoff advantage of the DIY route, though it comes with the ongoing configuration and maintenance responsibility that follows launch, which isn't part of the "time to launch" number but is very much part of the real timeline for having a properly functioning app.

## Custom development timelines

A fully custom app built by a development agency or freelance team from a blank slate typically takes the longest, often several months depending on scope, since every screen, integration, and feature is built without the head start a builder or a scoped done-for-you process provides. This timeline reflects the tradeoff for maximum flexibility.

## Done-for-you timelines

A done-for-you build like Bonsify's runs on a scoped timeline determined during the free audit, since the process, audit, build, launch, and an optional support retainer, is structured but not templated. The audit itself takes a few days and determines the actual build timeline based on catalog complexity, feature requirements, and whether the store needs iOS, Android, or both. This tends to land faster than fully custom development, since the build process and architecture decisions are already established, but slower than a template builder, since the app is native rather than assembled from pre-built components.

## What actually affects the timeline, regardless of model

A few factors move build time up or down across any approach: catalog size and complexity, whether push notification strategy and install-to-purchase attribution need to be built from scratch or can use existing infrastructure, how many custom features beyond standard Shopify checkout are involved, and app store review time, which varies and isn't fully controllable by the development team regardless of how fast the build itself goes.

## What happens after "launch"

Timeline conversations often stop at "app goes live," but a newly launched app with no push strategy, no ASO, and no attribution tracking isn't actually finished in a meaningful sense, it's published but not yet set up to perform.
