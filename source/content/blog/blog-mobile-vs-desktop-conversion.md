Mobile conversion on Shopify runs meaningfully lower than desktop conversion, even though mobile carries most of the traffic. About 75 percent of ecommerce traffic is mobile, and it drives roughly 57 percent of sales (Statista, 2025), a gap that shows the imbalance directly: mobile brings the visitors, desktop closes a disproportionate share of the purchases.

## The numbers behind the gap

Mobile web converts at roughly 2.1 to 2.9 percent, against 3.5 to 3.9 percent on desktop (Dynamic Yield, 2025). That is not a small difference. It means a store getting three-quarters of its traffic from phones is converting that majority of visitors at a meaningfully lower rate than the smaller desktop share, which is exactly backwards from what traffic volume alone would predict.

Cart abandonment tells the same story from a different angle. Mobile web abandonment runs around 85 percent, compared to roughly 20 percent inside apps (Barilliance; Venn Apps). That 65-point spread is where a lot of the mobile conversion gap actually lives, not at the top of the funnel where customers arrive, but at the bottom where they're supposed to check out.

## Why phones specifically leak sales

A few things compound on mobile that don't on desktop. Screens are smaller, so product comparison, size charts, and detailed research take more taps and more scrolling. Checkout forms that are tolerable on a full keyboard become frustrating on a phone keyboard, especially for address and payment fields. And browser tabs are easy to abandon mid-session, a text message or a notification pulls attention away, and the tab doesn't always get reopened.

None of this means mobile customers are less interested in buying. It means the mobile web experience, as most Shopify stores run it, adds friction that desktop doesn't have, and that friction shows up directly in the conversion numbers.

## What actually closes the gap

Site speed matters more on mobile than desktop, since mobile networks and processing power are more variable. A theme built for speed, not just visual polish, recovers some of the gap on its own. Checkout simplification, fewer fields, saved payment methods, autofill support, addresses another chunk.

The bigger lever is moving customers off the mobile browser entirely. Native apps convert about 130 percent higher than mobile web (Venn Apps, 2025), and users spend 6.4 times longer per session inside them (BuildFire), because the software isn't fighting against browser tab-switching and small-screen checkout friction the same way a website is. Push notifications, running 20 to 26 percent open rates against 15 to 20 percent for email, also give stores a direct line to customers that a mobile browser tab can't replicate.

## FAQ

**Why does mobile convert lower than desktop if it has more traffic?**
Smaller screens, harder checkout forms, and easier tab abandonment all add friction that desktop browsing doesn't have to the same degree, even though mobile brings the majority of visitors.

**Is the mobile-desktop gap the same across all product categories?**
No. It varies significantly, from an 71 percent gap in food and beverage to a 180 percent gap in furniture (Eevy AI, 2026), depending on how considered the purchase decision typically is.

**Does a faster mobile theme close the gap on its own?**
It helps, but usually only partially. The larger gains tend to come from moving customers into a native app or PWA that avoids mobile browser friction entirely.

**How much does cart abandonment differ between mobile web and apps?**
Roughly 85 percent on mobile web versus 20 percent inside apps (Barilliance; Venn Apps), one of the widest gaps in ecommerce.
