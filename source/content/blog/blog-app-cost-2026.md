The cost of a Shopify mobile app in 2026 depends heavily on which model a store chooses: a self-serve builder subscription, a custom development project, or a done-for-you build, and those three models are priced on entirely different bases, which makes direct comparison harder than it looks.

## Self-serve builder pricing

Template app builders run on monthly subscriptions, generally starting anywhere from $49 to $200 a month depending on the platform and tier. MageNative starts around $49 a month, Shopney around $99, Vajro (now Superfans) around $100 with scaling fees as volume grows, Plobal Apps around $199 targeting larger merchants. These prices cover access to a builder dashboard, not the labor of configuring and maintaining the app, which typically falls to someone on the merchant's own team as an ongoing responsibility. Several of these platforms also add success fees or scaling charges once a store passes certain volume thresholds, so the advertised starting price isn't always the full picture at scale.

## Custom development pricing

Hiring a development agency or freelance team to build a fully custom app from scratch is the most expensive route by a significant margin, and it's priced as a project rather than a subscription, often running into the tens of thousands of dollars depending on scope, with ongoing costs for any future changes billed separately. It makes sense for genuinely unusual technical requirements, but for most Shopify stores it costs more than the problem requires.

## Done-for-you pricing

A done-for-you build sits between the two models. It's native software, not a template, which puts the build quality closer to custom development, but it's scoped and delivered as a defined project rather than open-ended agency billing. Bonsify's process starts with a free audit that produces a fixed, scoped quote before any commitment, so cost isn't a guess based on a generic price list, it reflects the specific store's catalog, features, and requirements. There are no success fees or GMV percentages layered on afterward, so the number doesn't move as the store scales the way some builder tiers do.

## What actually drives the cost

Regardless of the model, a few factors move the price: catalog complexity, whether push notification strategy and install-to-purchase attribution are built in from the start, whether the build is iOS only, Android only, or both, and whether ongoing maintenance is included or handled separately. A store with a large, variant-heavy catalog and specific integration needs costs more to build well than a simple catalog with standard Shopify checkout, across any of the three models.

## How to think about cost versus return

The lowest sticker price isn't always the lowest total cost. A $49-a-month builder that nobody on the team has time to maintain properly, or that underperforms because push and attribution were never set up right, can cost more in lost conversion than a higher upfront quote for a build that's actually configured to convert. Native apps convert about 130 percent higher than mobile web (Venn Apps, 2025), a number that only shows up when the app is built and set up well, not just live.
