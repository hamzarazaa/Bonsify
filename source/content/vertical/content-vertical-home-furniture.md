## Why furniture has the widest mobile gap of any category

Furniture and home purchases involve measuring rooms, checking dimensions, comparing materials, and often visualizing how a piece fits a space, none of which mobile browsers handle well. That deliberation, combined with a $200 average order value, makes furniture the single most expensive category to lose customers in through mobile friction. Cart abandonment runs around 85 percent on mobile web versus roughly 20 percent inside apps (Barilliance; Venn Apps), and at furniture's price point, that gap represents real revenue lost to a slow checkout, not a minor inconvenience.

## What a native app changes for home and furniture brands

Native apps convert about 130 percent higher than mobile web (Venn Apps, 2025), and users spend 6.4 times longer per session inside them (BuildFire), directly relevant for a category where customers return repeatedly to compare pieces before committing. A faster, native browsing and comparison experience keeps that research inside the app instead of a customer abandoning mid-decision on a slow mobile site. Push notifications, running 20 to 26 percent open rates against 15 to 20 percent for email, work well for home and furniture around price drops, restocks on popular pieces, and new collection launches.
