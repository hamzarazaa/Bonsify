## Why push strategy matters more than push access

Most app builders include some form of push notification capability, but access to the feature isn't the same as a working strategy. A notification sent to every customer at the same time, with generic copy and no segmentation, underperforms the category average badly, and can push customers to disable notifications entirely. A notification tied to a specific behavior, a cart left behind, an item back in stock, sent to the right customer at the right time, is what produces the 20 to 26 percent open rate range.

Bonsify builds this into the app during the launch phase, using the store's actual order and customer data rather than generic templates. That includes deciding which events warrant a push at all, since notification fatigue is a real risk that undermines the channel if every event triggers a message.

## FAQ

**What triggers should a Shopify app's push notifications be based on?**
Real store events like abandoned checkout, back-in-stock, order status updates, and price drops tend to perform best, rather than generic promotional blasts.

**How often should push notifications be sent?**
Cadence depends on the store's customer behavior and is planned as part of the send calendar to avoid notification fatigue.

**Can push notification strategy be added to an existing app?**
Yes, that scope is defined during the free audit for stores with an app already built elsewhere.

**How is push notification performance measured?**
Through install-to-purchase attribution, which ties notification sends to actual purchase behavior rather than just open rates.

**Do PWAs support the same push strategy as native apps?**
PWAs support push notifications with more platform limitations than native apps, which the free audit accounts for when recommending a format.
